Data and limits

🕳️ Black Hole Lab

Mass: M☉

Your distance: × rs

Anatomy

Event horizon r_s = 2GM/c². Photon sphere 1.5 r_s. Innermost stable circular orbit 3 r_s.

Tidal stretch across length L: a = d/dr(GM/r²)·L = 2GM·L/r³. It decides if a crew survives before the horizon.

Real milestones

1915–16 General relativity; Schwarzschild solution.
1974 Hawking radiation predicted.
2015 LIGO detects merging black holes.
2019 / 2022 Event Horizon Telescope images M87* and Sgr A*.

Escape velocity

At radius r from a mass M: v_esc = √(2GM/r). At the Schwarzschild radius this equals c. Inside, nothing — not even light — escapes.

📐 Maths Guide

Parametric curves

Orbit: r(t) = a(cos nt, sin nt), n = 2π/T. Speed is |r′(t)| = na.

Line integral = risk

R = ∫ h(r(s)) ds where h is the hazard field. The agent compares R for three routes.

Gradient

∇h points toward increasing danger. The safest route is one that keeps |∇h| low and avoids its peaks.

Calculus of variations

Minimise ∫(1 + λh) ds over all paths. Solved numerically with Dijkstra on a 140×140 grid, a discrete eikonal solver.

Orbital transfer

Vis-viva v² = μ(2/r − 1/a); Hohmann time T = π√(a³/μ) from Kepler's law.

Tsiolkovsky equation

Δv = v_e ∫dm/m = v_e ln(m₀/m_f). Integrating thrust gives fuel needed.

Derivatives in relativity

Time dilation √(1 − r_s/r), Lorentz factor 1/√(1 − v²/c²).

Synodic period

1/S = |1/T₁ − 1/T₂|. The time between successive launch windows for a Hohmann transfer between two planets.
