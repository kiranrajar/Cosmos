📐 Maths Guide · How Space Math Works

Discover the exciting formulas behind space exploration! From 3D roller-coaster curves to giant planet shapes and rocket equations, here is how each math superpower works in COSMOS.

🌀 3D Space Curves

Roller-Coaster Trajectory
r(t) = ⟨a·cos(nt), a·sin(nt), c·t⟩ Speed |r′(t)| = √(n²a² + c²)

The Big Idea: Imagine riding a spiraling roller-coaster through outer space! Parametric equations tell your spaceship exactly where to be $(x, y, z)$ at every second of time $t$.

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Steers our real-time 3D planetary orbits and draws smooth spacecraft flight paths around the Sun.

🪐 Ellipsoids

Squished Space Spheres
x²/a² + y²/b² + z²/c² = 1 x = a·cos(u)cos(v), y = b·cos(u)sin(v), z = c·sin(u)

The Big Idea: Not all planets are perfect round balls! Giant spinning gas planets like Jupiter bulge out at their equator and get squished like a spinning water balloon.

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Accurately builds 3D models of rotating planets with realistic equatorial bulges instead of plain flat circles.

📡 Paraboloids

Satellite Dish & Escape Paths
z = x²/a² + y²/b² Escape Velocity v = √(2GM / r)

The Big Idea: Think of a giant shiny satellite dish that catches whisper-quiet signals from millions of miles away. It also draws the exact threshold curve needed to break free from the Sun's gravity!

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Calculates the exact speed a probe needs to escape solar orbit, and models high-gain radio antenna communication dishes.

⚡ Hyperboloids

Gravity Slingshot Swings
x²/a² + y²/b² - z²/c² = 1 Hyperbolic excess velocity v_∞

The Big Idea: An hourglass-shaped curve! When a spacecraft zooms close to Jupiter, it swings along a hyperbolic path and gets catapulted into deep space like a pinball wizard.

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Powers Voyager 1's interstellar escape trajectory and shapes the spinning ergosphere around black holes.

🔦 Cones

Spacetime Flashlight Beams
z² = x²/a² + y²/b² Relativistic Light Cone: c²t² - r² = 0

The Big Idea: Shine a flashlight in the dark and you see a cone of light! In physics, light cones show all the possible places in the universe you could ever reach traveling at the speed of light.

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Maps sensor coverage areas and displays relativistic spacetime boundaries where nothing can travel faster than light.

🛡️ Line Integral

Danger Shield Accumulator
R = ∫ h(r(s)) ds Total Risk = Sum of (Hazard × Step Distance)

The Big Idea: Like adding up points on a treasure map! As your spaceship flies along its path, this formula adds up every bit of radiation and space dust you hit to see if your crew stays safe.

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Our Mission Analyst runs this integral to tell you if a route has too much solar radiation for astronauts to survive.

🧭 The Gradient

Danger Compass
∇h = ⟨∂h/∂x, ∂h/∂y⟩ Direction of Steepest Danger

The Big Idea: A magic compass that points directly toward the hottest fire! To stay completely safe, your pilot simply steers sideways or away from where $\nabla h$ is pointing.

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Steers your ship around Jupiter's lethal radiation belts and away from blistering solar corona flares.

🛣️ Calculus of Variations

Super-Smart Route Finder
Minimise ∫ (1 + λ·h) ds Solved with Dijkstra on 140×140 Grid

The Big Idea: Like Google Maps for the Solar System! Out of millions of possible zigzag routes through space, this calculus finds the single fastest and safest highway.

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Creates the cyan "Optimised Safe Corridor" path in the Mission Analyst map!

🚀 Vis-Viva & Transfer

Space Jump Energy
v² = μ · (2/r - 1/a) Hohmann Transfer Time T = π√(a³ / μ)

The Big Idea: How do we hop from Earth to Mars? We fire our engines once to stretch our orbit into a giant egg-shaped oval, coast peacefully across space, and fire again to brake!

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Computes the exact engine burn speeds (Δv₁ and Δv₂) and tells you the exact travel time in days.

⛽ Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation

Rocket Fuel Calculator
Δv = v_e · ln(m₀ / m_f) Fuel Mass Ratio = exp(Δv / v_e)

The Big Idea: The golden law of rocketry! The faster you want to go, the more fuel you must carry. But fuel is heavy, so carrying more fuel means you need even MORE fuel to lift it!

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Calculates whether chemical rocket fuel or high-tech ion beam propulsion can physically carry your ship to its destination.

⏳ Time Dilation

Einstein's Time Machine
τ = t · √(1 - r_s / r) Lorentz Factor γ = 1 / √(1 - v² / c²)

The Big Idea: Clocks tick slower near giant black holes and when flying near light speed! An astronaut could fly near a black hole for 1 hour while 10 years pass back on Earth!

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Calculates crew clocks in the Black Hole Lab and predicts astronaut aging on deep-space relativistic flights.

📅 Synodic Launch Window

Planetary Alignment Clock
1/S = |1/T₁ - 1/T₂| Waiting Time between Windows

The Big Idea: Earth and Mars are like two runners on a circular track. Earth runs faster on the inside lane! This formula calculates the exact day Earth catches up and lines up for the next launch jump.

🚀 How COSMOS uses it: Tells you the exact next launch departure date (e.g. Earth to Mars every 26 months / 780 days).
